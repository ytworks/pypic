# Achilleos et al. (2006): Orientation, location, and velocity of Saturn's bow shock: Initial results from the Cassini spacecraft

- Ref: JGR 111, A3; DOI 10.1029/2005JA011297 (11 authors: Achilleos, Bertucci, Russell, Hospodarsky, Rymer,
  Arridge, Burton, Dougherty, Hendricks, Smith, Tsurutani). Peer reviewed.
- Read: 2026-11-14, abstract only (full text not obtained); topics: qpar-electron-conditions

## Claim
The first Cassini crossings of Saturn's bow shock are mainly quasi-perpendicular, with a ramp about one ion
inertial length thick, and the shock surface moves fast.

## Method
- Magnetic field and plasma data of the crossings on the insertion orbit (from 1 July 2004); normals and
  speeds from the crossings.

## Numbers to compare against
- "Saturn bow shock velocities up to ~400 km/s."
- Ramp scale about an ion inertial length.

## Assessment
- An early sample from one orbit.
- For this topic: masters2013electron takes the typical upstream temperatures for M_s and M_f ~25 from
  this work, and masters2017situ cites it (with masters2011electron) for a shock motion of 100 km/s. Shock
  speeds up to ~400 km/s mean that the upstream speed in the shock frame of a single crossing can differ
  from the solar wind speed by a large fraction.
