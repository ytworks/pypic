# Cairns (1987a): The electron distribution function upstream from the Earth's bow shock

## Claim
"A general analytic theory for the distribution function of particles backstreaming from an arbitrary
shock in a magnetized plasma (neglecting wave-particle scattering)". It shows that "abrupt cutoffs in the
parallel velocity ... arise naturally in the upstream region, irrespective of the acceleration processes
active at the shock."

## Method
- Analytic theory without wave-particle scattering.
- "Two classes of cutoff arise, escape cutoffs due to the particles requiring a characteristic speed to
  outrun the shock, and edge cutoffs due to the shock having an edge."
- "Analytic expressions for the particle distribution function upstream of finite planar shocks and
  shocks whose two-dimensional sections are parabolic are derived."

## Numbers to compare against
- None in the abstract. It says the distribution functions and "the spatial variation of the cutoff
  velocity" are shown in figures.

## Assessment
- For this topic: direct prior work on the upstream electron distribution of a curved (parabolic) shock.
  The cutoff velocity varies in space, and it arises whatever the acceleration process is.
- An analysis of the electrons upstream of our sphere should compute this escape cutoff for a spherical
  front first, and read as a signature of the mechanism only what the cutoff does not explain.
- Limits in the abstract: no scattering; the acceleration at the shock is an input, not a result; the
  sections are parabolic, not spherical.
