## Claim
A parameter study with 2-D hybrid simulations finds the conditions under which laser-ablated debris in
the LAPD forms a perpendicular collisionless shock, and compares them to an analytical coupling
parameter.

## Method
- 2-D hybrid simulations of perpendicular shocks, modelled on the LAPD with the Raptor laser: carbon
  targets, 100-500 J on target, debris into partially ionised hydrogen or helium.

## Numbers to compare against
- The abstract gives no numbers: "Simulation results are presented along with a comparison to an
  analytical coupling parameter."
- Second-hand, Schaeffer et al. 2015, Sect. II: "Hybrid simulations [this paper] suggest that
  epsilon >~ 0.5 is necessary for shock formation", with epsilon = R_M^2/(rho_d rho_a).
- Second-hand, Schaeffer et al. 2015, Sect. I: hybrid work predicts that "the piston radius must be larger
  than a piston ion directed gyroradius" (their Ref. 26, this paper).

## Assessment
- For this topic: the origin of the coupling threshold used in the laboratory work. A design with
  R_m/rho_d = 6-8 is far above it (see the note on Schaeffer 2015).
- The study is about whether a shock forms at all in a weak-drive regime (M_A of a few), not about how
  strong it becomes. The Mach number of the shock against that of the debris must be taken from the
  full text.
- The full text should be read before the threshold is quoted as this paper's own result.
