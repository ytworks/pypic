## Claim
Debris that expands into an ambient plasma with a density gradient couples its energy to the ambient ions
better than in a uniform plasma. A shock forms and propagates inside the dense region.

## Method
- 2-D hybrid simulations of super-Alfvenic debris in an inhomogeneous ambient plasma.
- "an expanding cylinder running into a step function gradient", compared to a simple analytical theory.
- One run with a more realistic debris expansion and ambient density, compared to magnetic flux probe
  data of a laboratory shock experiment.

## Numbers to compare against
- The abstract gives no numbers. Statements: "improved collisionless coupling of energy to the ambient

## Assessment
- For this topic: a set-up trick. A jump to a denser ambient plasma lowers the local Alfven speed and the
  local ion scales, so the same piston is stronger there. This explanation is ours; the abstract states
  only the result.
- It costs the uniform upstream that the question of this topic needs: on a sphere in a uniform field
  the change of theta_Bn along the front is the subject, and a density step adds a second variation.
- An expanding cylinder in a hybrid code; nothing on electrons. The size of the gain is in the full text.
