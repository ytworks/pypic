# Cristofari, Blasi & Caprioli (2021): Cosmic ray protons and electrons from supernova remnants

- Ref: A&A 650, A62 (2021) (volume and article number from the Crossref record of the DOI);
  DOI 10.1051/0004-6361/202140448; arXiv:2103.02375 (v1, "Accepted for publication in Astronomy &
  Astrophysics"); peer-reviewed.
- Read: 2026-11-13, abstract; topics: spherical-shock-injection
- ABSTRACT ONLY (arXiv abstract). Nothing below goes beyond the abstract.

## Claim
With the post-shock field set by the cosmic-ray-driven non-resonant instability alone, the spectra of
electrons and protons released by a supernova remnant differ, "but such a difference becomes appreciable only
at energies >~ 100-1000 GeV, while observations of the electron spectrum require such a difference to be
present at energies as low as ~10 GeV".

## Method
- Calculation of the spectrum of protons released during the evolution of supernovae of different types, with
  escape from upstream and adiabatic losses of the particles advected downstream. The same for electrons.
- Post-shock field from "an analytic treatment of the magnetic field amplification due to non-resonant and
  resonant streaming instability and their saturation".

## Numbers to compare against
- Energy above which the released electron and proton spectra differ: >~ 100-1000 GeV in the model; ~10 GeV
  required by observations.
- An effect at ~10 GeV "requires substantial magnetic field amplification in the late stages" of the
  evolution, at shock velocity << 1000 km/s.

## Assessment
- For this topic: background only. It treats the released spectra of particles that are already accelerated,
  not the injection of electrons.
- The abstract ends by speculating that the difference between electrons and protons "may reflect either some
  unknown acceleration effect, or additional energy losses in cocoons around the sources". It names no such
  effect, so nothing here can be tied to the geometry of this topic.
