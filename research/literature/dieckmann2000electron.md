# Dieckmann, McClements, Chapman, Dendy & Drury (2000): Electron acceleration due to high frequency instabilities at supernova remnant shocks

- Ref: Astron. Astrophys. 356, 377 (2000) (journal reference of the arXiv record; the record has no DOI);
  arXiv:astro-ph/0002347 (v1); peer-reviewed.
- Read: 2026-11-13, abstract; topics: spherical-shock-injection
- ABSTRACT ONLY (arXiv abstract). Nothing below goes beyond the abstract.

## Claim
Ions reflected from a perpendicular shock front, "with speeds greater than the upstream electron thermal
speed", give the free energy to accelerate electrons "from background energies to tens of keV". The abstract
lists as new: "the acceleration, rather than heating, of electrons via the Buneman instability; the
acceleration of electrons to speeds exceeding those of the shock-reflected ions producing the instability;
and strong acceleration of electrons perpendicular to the magnetic field".

## Method
- "an electromagnetic particle-in-cell (PIC) code", for "perpendicular collisionless shocks associated with
  SNRs". Dimensionality, mass ratio and whether a full shock or only the beam-plasma system of the foot is
  simulated: not in the abstract.

## Numbers to compare against
- Electron energies reached: "tens of keV".
- "Electron energization takes place through a variety of resonant and non-resonant processes, of which the
  strongest involves stochastic wave-particle interactions."

## Assessment
- For this topic: an early PIC result on the Buneman route to electron pre-acceleration at a perpendicular
  shock, the theta_Bn = 90 deg end (the equator of our sphere). It is one of the mechanisms to test for there.
- The Buneman instability needs reflected ions faster than the upstream electron thermal speed. A run of
  this topic must state whether its shock meets that condition before it reads electron energization at the
  equator as this mechanism. (This reading is ours; the abstract gives the condition only.)
