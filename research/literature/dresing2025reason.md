# Dresing et al. (2025): On the reason for the widespread energetic storm particle event of 13 March 2023

- Ref: A&A 695, A127; DOI 10.1051/0004-6361/202453596; arXiv:2502.06332 (23 authors: Dresing, Jebaraj,
  Wijsen, Palmerio, Rodriguez-Garcia, Palmroos, Gieseler, Jarry, ...). Peer reviewed. Read in the arXiv
  version.
- Read: 2026-11-14, abstract and the passages on the PSP crossing and the shock parameters (arXiv HTML);
  the simulations and the other spacecraft skimmed; topics: qpar-electron-conditions

## Claim
The 2023-03-13 event was seen at six locations around the Sun, each with a shock crossing and an energetic
storm particle event. Two scenarios reproduce this in MHD simulations: one circumsolar blast-wave-like
shock, or several coronal mass ejections driving shocks in different directions. The blast wave fits the
arrival times slightly better.

## Method
- Data of seven missions (PSP, Solar Orbiter, BepiColombo, STEREO A, near-Earth spacecraft, MAVEN);
  magnetic connectivity; MHD solar wind simulations with the pre-event and the main ejections.

## Numbers to compare against
- PSP crossing as used there: theta_Bn 8 +- 4 deg, shock speed 2800 +- 300 km/s, M_A 9.1 +- 1.35, density
  compression 4 (the values of jebaraj2024acceleration).
- PSP crossed the flank of the shock, not its nose.
- The low-energy particle instrument at PSP was saturated near the shock.
- A series of ejections preceded the event.

## Assessment
- For this topic: the PSP shock is one part of a global structure with a history (H7). The upstream
  medium had been crossed by earlier ejections (H4, H5), and the instrument saturation limits the
  low-energy spectrum (H8).
- The shock parameters here are the 2024 ones; later works revise them (jebaraj2026wave: M_A 7-7.5;
  giacalone2025source: M_A ~4, 1350 km/s; chiappetta2026evolution: theta_Bn ~20 deg, M_A 4.7 +- 3.5).
- Both scenarios fit: the paper does not decide what the shock was near the Sun.
