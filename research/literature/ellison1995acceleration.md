# Ellison, Baring & Jones (1995): Acceleration rates and injection efficiencies in oblique shocks

- Ref: Astrophys. J. 453, 873 (1995); DOI 10.1086/176447; peer-reviewed.
- Read: 2026-11-13, abstract; topics: spherical-shock-injection
- ABSTRACT ONLY (OpenAlex record). The full text was not read. Nothing below goes beyond the abstract.

## Claim
The first-order Fermi rate rises with obliquity, but the injection of thermal particles falls: "shocks
which accelerate particles most rapidly are least capable of injecting thermal particles into the
acceleration process."

## Method
- Test-particle Monte Carlo simulation with large-angle scattering; particle orbits are computed exactly
  in "shocked, laminar, nonrelativistic flows" (earlier work of the authors assumed magnetic moment
  conservation).
- The injection efficiency is computed as a function of obliquity, Mach number and degree of turbulence.

## Numbers to compare against
- Rates at quasi-perpendicular shocks "can be hundreds of times higher than those seen in parallel
  shocks".
- No injection efficiencies are given in the abstract.

## Assessment
- For this topic: a statement of the trade-off between rate and injection across obliquity.
  "turbulence must be quite strong for high Mach number, highly oblique shocks to inject significant
  numbers of thermal particles".
- The scattering law is assumed, and the abstract speaks of "thermal particles", not of electrons.
  Background for the electron mechanism.
