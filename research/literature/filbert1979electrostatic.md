# Filbert & Kellogg (1979): Electrostatic noise at the plasma frequency beyond the Earth's bow shock

- Ref: J. Geophys. Res. 84(A4), 1369–1381 (1979); DOI 10.1029/JA084iA04p01369; peer-reviewed.
- Read: 2026-11-13, abstract; topics: spherical-shock-injection
- ABSTRACT ONLY (OpenAlex record). The full text was not read. Nothing below goes beyond the abstract.

## Claim
Electrostatic noise near the plasma frequency upstream of the bow shock is tied to magnetic connection to
the shock: "(1) the noise is present when the field lines connect to the shock, and no noise at the plasma
frequency is observed when the field lines do not connect and (2) the noise amplitude is largest along
field lines that are tangent to the shock."

## Method
- Plasma wave data of the Imp 6 satellite, sorted by whether the interplanetary field "probably connects
  to the shock".
- Interpretation: a two-stream instability. The beam comes from "a finite time of flight mechanism; that
  is, of the energetic electrons generated in the shock, the lower-energy electrons are swept downstream
  by the solar wind convection field, leaving a higher-energy bump on the distribution function."
- Theory of saturation: the oscillating two-stream mechanism in the sunward part, quasilinear diffusion
  in the downstream part.

## Numbers to compare against
- None in the abstract (no amplitudes, energies or angles).

## Assessment
- For this topic: upstream of a curved shock, the noise that the authors attribute to shock electrons is
  strongest on the field lines tangent to the front. A tangent field line touches the front where
  theta_Bn is 90 deg; on our sphere that is the equator.
- The time-of-flight filter shapes the upstream distribution by convection alone: the slower electrons
  are swept downstream, and a bump is left. An upstream beam in our run needs the same care before it is
  read as a signature of the mechanism.
- The source is "energetic electrons generated in the shock"; the abstract names no acceleration process.
