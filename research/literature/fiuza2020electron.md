# Fiuza, Swadling, Grassi, Rinderknecht, Higginson, Ryutov, Bruulsema, Drake et al. (2020): Electron acceleration in laboratory-produced turbulent collisionless shocks

- Ref: Nat. Phys. 16, 916-920; DOI 10.1038/s41567-020-0919-4. Peer reviewed. 20 authors.
- Read: 2026-11-14, abstract (journal page); topics: qpar-electron-conditions

## Claim
"Here we report results from laser-driven plasma flow experiments, and related simulations, that probe the
formation of turbulent collisionless shocks in conditions relevant to young supernova remnants. We show that
electrons can be effectively accelerated in a first-order Fermi process by small-scale turbulence produced
within the shock transition to relativistic non-thermal energies, helping overcome the injection problem."

## Method
- Laser-driven counter-streaming plasma flows at the National Ignition Facility, with simulations.

## Numbers to compare against
- From `orusa2025criteria` (its survey; not in the abstract): non-thermal electrons to ~500 keV, more than
  100 times the thermal energy.

## Assessment
- The shocks are Weibel-mediated, without an ordered upstream field of the kind our cases have.
- For this topic: the one place where electron injection at a collisionless shock has been measured in a
  controlled system and compared with kinetic simulations (see also `grassi2023electron`, which takes a
  diffusion coefficient from tracked electrons for this experiment). It is a third kind of confrontation
  beside spacecraft events and remote emission. The regime is a turbulent transition without an ordered
  field; of our cases only Saturn's (M_A ~100) is at a comparably high Mach number, and it has an ordered
  field at 20°.
