# Fulbright & Reynolds (1990): Bipolar supernova remnants and the obliquity dependence of shock acceleration

- Ref: Astrophys. J. 357, 591 (1990); DOI 10.1086/168947; peer-reviewed.
- Read: 2026-11-13, abstract; topics: spherical-shock-injection
- ABSTRACT ONLY (OpenAlex record; the wording reads like an abstracting-service summary). The full text was
  not read. Nothing below goes beyond the abstract.

## Claim
The efficiency of diffusive shock acceleration depends on theta_Bn, the angle between the shock normal and
a uniform magnetic field: "models with theta(Bn) of about 90 deg can reproduce the observed azimuthal
intensity ratios in most cases, but models with theta(Bn) near 0 deg cannot."

## Method
- Numerical models of "young adiabatically expanding shell SNRs" in a uniform field; the output is
  synthetic radio maps, compared with the bipolarity of the observed synchrotron radio emission.
- The form of the obliquity dependence put into the models is not in the abstract.

## Numbers to compare against
- None in the abstract (the azimuthal intensity ratios are named, not given).

## Assessment
- For this topic: it supports the "equatorial belt" reading of bilateral radio remnants: bright limbs
  where the shock is quasi-perpendicular. The abstracts of `rothenflug2004geometry` and
  `volk2003variation` point the other way (polar caps).
- It is a statement about acceleration efficiency at the scale of a remnant, from radio maps. The abstract
  has no kinetic electron physics and no injection mechanism. Background for the mechanism question.
