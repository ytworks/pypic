# Gaensler (1998): The nature of bilateral supernova remnants

- Ref: Astrophys. J. 493, 781–792 (1998); DOI 10.1086/305146; arXiv:astro-ph/9707149; peer-reviewed.
- Read: 2026-11-13, abstract; topics: spherical-shock-injection
- ABSTRACT ONLY (OpenAlex record). The full text was not read. Nothing below goes beyond the abstract.

## Claim
The axes of bilateral remnants follow the Galactic plane: in a sample of 17 there is "a highly significant
tendency for the bilateral axes of these SNRs to be aligned with the Galactic plane". The cause is
"extrinsic": the Galactic magnetic field, "either directly, in the form of magnetic field compression
and/or quasi-perpendicular acceleration of electrons in the supernova shock, or more likely indirectly, by
preprocessing the interstellar medium to produce density stratifications extended along the plane."

## Method
- Radio images at 1.4 GHz of G003.8-00.3 and G350.0-02.0, and a sample of 17 bilateral remnants.

## Numbers to compare against
- Sample size 17. The significance value is not in the abstract.

## Assessment
- For this topic: the radio morphology ties the axis to the ambient field, but the author prefers density
  stratification over an obliquity dependence of electron acceleration. So bilateral radio shells alone do
  not decide between the polar-cap and the equatorial-belt reading.
- Background for the mechanism question.
