# Giacalone (2017): The acceleration of charged particles at a spherical shock moving through an irregular magnetic field

- Ref: Astrophys. J. 848, 123 (2017); DOI 10.3847/1538-4357/aa8df1; erratum Astrophys. J. 898, 93 (2020);
  peer-reviewed.
- Read: 2026-11-13, abstract; topics: spherical-shock-injection

  and no open copy was found. Nothing below goes beyond the abstract.

## Claim
Test protons at a spherical blast wave moving into a uniform mean field with turbulence are most intense
at the poles (quasi-parallel) of the blast wave, although the highest-energy protons are first accelerated
near the equator (quasi-perpendicular): "The particles 'collect' at the poles as they approximately adhere
to magnetic field lines that move poleward from their initial encounter with the shock at the equator, as
the shock expands. The field lines at the poles have been connected to the shock the longest."

## Method
- Test PROTONS (not electrons); orbits integrated in electric and magnetic fields from a kinematically
  defined flow of a radially propagating blast wave; the field inside from Maxwell's equations.
- Upstream: uniform mean field plus an irregular component with a Kolmogorov-like spectrum.
- Shock speed law, radius in gyroradii, turbulence amplitude: not in the abstract.

- None in the abstract.

## Assessment
- For this topic: the nearest published statement of the cross-latitude history at a spherically expanding
  shock: a field line first meets the front at the equator (theta_Bn = 90 deg), its contact point then
  moves poleward, and particles energized at high theta_Bn are found later at low theta_Bn. It is for
  protons, with a prescribed shock and with turbulence (diffusive / drift acceleration over many
  gyroperiods), not for electron injection from the thermal pool.
- This paper must be read in full before the topic claims novelty for the same history for electrons:
  whether the blast wave decelerates, and what is said on energy against the latitude of first contact,
  is not known from the abstract.
