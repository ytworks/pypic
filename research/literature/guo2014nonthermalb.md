# Guo, Sironi & Narayan (2014): Non-Thermal Electron Acceleration in Low Mach Number Collisionless Shocks. II. Firehose-Mediated Fermi Acceleration and its Dependence on Pre-Shock Conditions

- Ref: ApJ 797, 47 (2014); DOI 10.1088/0004-637X/797/1/47; arXiv:1409.7393 (v2, "accepted to ApJ");
  peer-reviewed. Part I is `guo2014nonthermal`.
- Read: 2026-11-13, abstract; topics: spherical-shock-injection
- ABSTRACT ONLY (arXiv abstract). Nothing below goes beyond the abstract.

## Claim
The upstream oblique waves that scatter electrons back to a low Mach number quasi-perpendicular shock (the
Fermi-like process of part I) "are generated by the shock-reflected electrons via the firehose instability,
which is driven by an anisotropy in the electron velocity distribution". The mechanism "works for shocks
with high plasma beta (>20) at nearly all magnetic field obliquities, and for electron temperatures in the
range relevant for galaxy clusters".

## Method
- 2-D PIC simulations of low Mach number (M = 3) shocks.
- A systematic scan of "the magnetic field obliquity, the flow magnetization (or equivalently, the plasma
  beta), and the upstream electron temperature". The scanned values are not in the abstract.

## Numbers to compare against
- Mach number M = 3 (part I); the class is M < 5.
- The mechanism works for plasma beta > 20, "at nearly all magnetic field obliquities".
- Acceleration efficiencies and spectral indices: not in the abstract.

## Assessment
- For this topic: a mechanism in which each energy gain is a shock drift cycle and the return to the shock
  is by waves that the reflected electrons drive themselves. It is one candidate at the quasi-perpendicular
  part of a front.
- Its regime is high beta (> 20) and low Mach number: galaxy clusters. The abstract does not say what
  happens at lower beta. A run of this topic at low beta cannot assume this mechanism; it would have to show
  the upstream waves and the electron anisotropy.
