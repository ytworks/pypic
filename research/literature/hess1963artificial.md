# Hess (1963): The artificial radiation belt made on July 9, 1962

- Ref: J. Geophys. Res. 68(3), 667–683 (1963); DOI 10.1029/JZ068i003p00667; peer-reviewed.
- Read: 2026-11-13, abstract; topics: spherical-shock-injection
- ABSTRACT ONLY (OpenAlex record). The full text was not obtained. Nothing below goes beyond the abstract.
- The same JGR issue holds a set of papers on this belt (OpenAlex lists "Collected Papers on the Artificial
  Radiation Belt from the July 9 1962, Nuclear Detonation", JGR 68, 605); a dissenting reading is
  `vanallen1963satellite`.

## Claim
"The available information on the artificial radiation belt formed by the July 9, 1962, high-altitude
nuclear explosion is reviewed. Data from Injun (1961o2), Telstar 1 (1962αε1), Traac (1961αη2), and Ariel 1
(1962o1) are combined to form one picture of the artificial belt."

## Method
- Review and combination of satellite particle data from four spacecraft into one flux map; the map is used
  to compute the flux met by other satellites and compared with solar-cell damage; "crude calculations of
  shielding".

## Numbers to compare against
- "The data are consistent to about a factor of 3."

## Assessment
- Shown (per the abstract): the explosion (Starfish) left a belt of trapped particles, mapped from four
  satellites to within a factor of 3. The abstract says nothing on how the electrons were put on trapped
  orbits or on their energy spectrum.
- Contested at the time: Van Allen, Frank & O'Brien 1963 (`vanallen1963satellite`) find their data "in
  marked disagreement" with the Hess–Nakada interpretation.
- For this topic (our reading): the only real case found of an explosion inside a planet's own dipole that
  left energetic electrons trapped on closed field lines. The trapped electrons there are mainly fission
  decay products (Pieper 1963, JGR 68, 651, seen only as an OpenAlex record, gives that origin for a second
  belt), so it is not evidence of shock acceleration; it bounds what "trapped after an explosion" means.
