## Claim
A super-Alfvenic plasma expansion can couple strongly to a magnetised background without collisions, but
small changes of the initial conditions let the debris ions decouple and slip through the background.

## Method
- "Fully 3-D Kinetic Ion Simulation Modeling (KISM)" of high-altitude nuclear explosions, guided by 1-D
  simulations. Kinetic ions; the abstract does not describe the electron model.

## Numbers to compare against
- "A slight increase in the ion charge density of the background plasma allows the debris ions to decouple
  and slip through the magnetized background. These decoupled ions in the expanding plasma then follow
  trajectories typical of single particle motion."
- Two thresholds for the onset of decoupling: "The first threshold depends on the ratio of the charge
  density of the expanding plasma to that of the background plasma. The second threshold is evident when
  the expanding plasma has a finite pulse length comparable to the gyroradius of the energized background
  ions."
- The abstract gives no values for the thresholds.

## Assessment
- For this topic: the second threshold is the finite-piston problem in other words. A debris pulse that
  is not longer than the gyroradius of the picked-up ambient ions does not hold them.
- A thin debris shell is at risk here. A heavy piston is safer only if its shell is also thicker than
  that gyroradius; our design should state the shell thickness in rho_d. This is our reading.
- Le et al. 2021 (Sect. 4) place this "decoupled" regime roughly at rho_d > R_m.
- The threshold values and the 3-D box are in the full text, which was not read.
