# Jebaraj, Dresing, Krasnoselskikh, Agapitov, Gieseler, Trotta, et al. (2023): Relativistic electron beams accelerated by an interplanetary shock

- Ref: Astron. Astrophys. 680, L7 (2023); DOI 10.1051/0004-6361/202348120; arXiv:2311.05765; peer-reviewed (letter).
- Read: 2026-11-13, skimmed (ar5iv full text, via extraction); topics: shock-alfvenic-turbulence

## Claim
Solar Orbiter saw field-aligned beams of electrons from ~30 keV up to ~1 MeV about 14 min ahead of an
interplanetary shock at 0.98 au — the first such relativistic beams upstream of an IP shock. Velocity dispersion
places their source ~13 R☉ away, on a remote, near-perpendicular part of the shock; a single-reflection
relativistic fast-Fermi model reproduces the spectral shape and break energy roughly.

## Method
- Event 2022-07-25, shock at 06:22:45 UT (06:22:47 in Fig. 1 caption); EPD/EPT (30–300 keV) and HET (>400 keV)
  at 1 s cadence; beams 06:06:00–06:08:30 UT.
- Shock parameters from single-spacecraft methods; M_A from proxies of Gedalin et al. (2021).
- Velocity-dispersion analysis on the first beam peaks (App. A.3, Fig. 7).
- Semi-analytic fast-Fermi model (Sect. 4, App. B): kappa upstream distribution, μ conservation, 1-D profile.

## Numbers to compare against
- Local shock: θBn ≈ 64° ± 6°; M_A ≈ 3.3 ± 0.7; V_sh ≈ 775 ± 50 km/s (observer frame); upstream/downstream
  normal flow in shock frame ≈ 350/125 km/s; r_B ≈ 3, r_gas ≳ 2.1. β, M_fms and upstream δB/B not reported.
- Beam injection 06:05:46 UT, source path ~13 R☉ (≈ 6 % of 1 au).
- Spectra (Fig. 2): power law with cutoff, δ = −1.19 ± 0.14 (peak 1), −1.96 ± 0.54 (peak 2), Ec ≈ 200 keV;
  broken power law on 2.5-min average δ1 = −1.4, δ2 = −5.2, E_b ≈ 262 ± 47 keV.
- Model inputs: κ ≈ 3–4, θBn 87°–89.5°, B_u ≈ 10 nT, overshoot ratio 7, cross-shock potential ~100 eV;
  model E_b ≈ 190–400 keV over that θBn range; acceleration time ~ electron gyroperiod (Eq. 17).

## Assessment
- Shown: relativistic electron beams exist upstream of a weak (M_A ≈ 3) IP shock, produced elsewhere on the
  front; the local shock (θBn ≈ 64°) is not the accelerator.
- The near-perpendicular remote geometry (θBn > 87°) is inferred, not measured; the authors invoke large-scale
  shock curvature/rippling to produce it (Sect. 5). Upstream fluctuations are not quantified; no strong wave
  activity near the beams.
- Limits stated: 1-D profile, μ conservation, single reflection; ion contamination corrections; second-peak
  onsets unreliable; break type (cutoff vs broken power law) not distinguishable.
- Relevance: motivates a mechanism that creates locally near-perpendicular, supercritical patches on a weak,
  oblique IP shock — which large-amplitude upstream turbulence could do (open, not tested here).
