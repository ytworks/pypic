# Jokipii & Giacalone (2007): Adiabatic compression acceleration of fast charged particles

- Ref: Astrophys. J. 660, 336–340 (2007); DOI 10.1086/513064; peer-reviewed.
- Read: 2026-11-13, abstract; topics: spherical-shock-injection
- ABSTRACT ONLY (OpenAlex record). The full text was not read. Nothing below goes beyond the abstract.

## Claim
A scatter-free mode of acceleration at shocks and compressions: fast, low-rigidity particles "can follow
the random magnetic field lines, which meander back and forth across the compression. Because of the
difference between the downstream and upstream flow speeds, the particles can be accelerated, much as in
standard diffusive shock acceleration." Pitch-angle scattering is not needed.

## Method
- Theory. The acceleration needs a disturbance that "propagates normal to a magnetic field which is
  turbulent on large scales", and particle speeds "much greater than the speed of the disturbance".
- Whether simulations support it is not in the abstract.

## Numbers to compare against
- None in the abstract.

## Assessment
- For this topic: a mechanism in which the shape of the field lines across the front, not scattering,
  returns electrons to the shock. The authors suggest it "may accelerate low-energy superthermal
  electrons, for which resonant scattering may not be possible".
- It needs field lines that cross the front more than once. In the abstract that comes from large-scale
  turbulence of the field, not from curvature of the shock. Whether a curved front in a uniform field
  gives the same multiple crossings is our question, not a statement of the abstract.
