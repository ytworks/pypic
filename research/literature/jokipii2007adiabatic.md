## Claim
Fast, low-rigidity particles (speed much above the shock speed) can be accelerated at a shock or compression that
moves normal to a mean field that is turbulent on large scales, without any pitch-angle scattering: they follow
random field lines that meander back and forth across the compression and gain energy from the difference of
upstream and downstream flow speeds, much as in diffusive shock acceleration.

## Assessment
- This is the original statement of the mechanism our topic tests: multiple connection of a convected, meandering
  field line to the front, scatter-free. The abstract names superthermal electrons, which cannot scatter
  resonantly, as the target, and proposes the process as a pre-injection stage up to energies where ion-driven
  waves can scatter them.
- Requirements implied by the abstract: particle speed >> shock speed (electrons above thermal speed for a
  nonrelativistic shock), field-line meandering on scales large compared with the gyroradius, and lines convected
  through the front. Its title calls it "adiabatic compression" acceleration; whether the paper treats the
  magnetic moment as conserved, and what spectrum or rate it derives, is not known from the abstract.
- Test-particle follow-ups: Guo & Giacalone (2010, 2012, 2015). Ion counterpart in a self-consistent hybrid run:
  Giacalone (2005, ApJL 628, L37).
- Re-read the paper before quoting a formula or a rate from it.
