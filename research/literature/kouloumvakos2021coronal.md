## Claim
"Our results suggest that the formation and evolution of a strong, supercritical, quasi-perpendicular shock
wave interacting with a coronal streamer were responsible for producing type II radio emission."

## Method
- 3D reconstruction and modelling of the shock of the 2014 November 5 event; spatial and temporal evolution
  of the shock properties compared with the type II emission.
- Background stated in the abstract: "the shock geometry and closed field line topology, e.g.,
  quasi-perpendicular shock regions or shocks interacting with streamers, play an important role".

## Numbers to compare against
- No values in the abstract. Qualitative: the shock is "subcritical before and supercritical after the start
  of the type II emission"; geometry "mostly quasi-perpendicular throughout the event".

## Assessment
- Shown (per the abstract): for one event, the type II starts when the front turns supercritical and comes
  from oblique to quasi-perpendicular parts that interact with a streamer.
- One event; electron acceleration is inferred from the radio emission, not modelled.
- For this topic (our reading): observational support that the closed (streamer) part of the field is where
  a coronal shock makes radio-emitting electrons. It does not separate the role of the closed topology
  (trap between two shock crossings) from that of theta_Bn and criticality.
