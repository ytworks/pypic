# Krauss-Varban & Burgess (1991): Electron acceleration at nearly perpendicular collisionless shocks: 2. Reflection at curved shocks

- Ref: J. Geophys. Res. 96(A1), 143–154 (1991); DOI 10.1029/90JA01728; peer-reviewed.
- Read: 2026-11-13, abstract; topics: spherical-shock-injection
- ABSTRACT ONLY (OpenAlex record). The full text was not obtained: the publisher host refused the request
  and no open copy was found. Nothing below goes beyond the abstract.

## Claim
At a curved shock an individual electron gains less energy than at a plane shock, but the reflected flux
is largely restored by two-dimensional focusing: "Electrons that drift into the shock over a wide area
converge and stream out within a narrow spatial area, thus greatly enhancing the flux of reflected
electrons."

## Method
- 2-D test-particle electrons; shock profile from hybrid simulations, "modified to include the first order
  effects of a global curvature in the vicinity of theta_Bn = 90 deg". The reflected flux is evaluated as a
  function of theta_Bn on the shock surface.

## Numbers to compare against
- Simple estimates: curvature limits the acceleration "above several keV in case of the Earth's bow shock".
- A kappa = 6 seed distribution gives the observed bow-shock fluxes up to 10–15 keV with curvature
  included; above that the observed spectra are harder.
- Low energies: the fluxes show a cut-off and a maximum (bump-on-tail in the foreshock).

## Assessment
- For this topic: the direct predecessor for "electrons enter over a wide range of positions on a curved
  front, drift, and leave in a narrow region". By the abstract, the individual gain is lower, not higher,
  than at a plane shock; the flux, not the energy, is enhanced. The size of the drift along the front, the
  exit theta_Bn and the model parameters (R_c, thickness, M_A) are in the full text, which must be read
  before this topic states what the paper did or did not show.
- Vandas 1994 (read in full) uses the same local model of curvature, B_n = B_1 z / R_c, and credits this
  paper as the first treatment of a curved shock for electrons.
