# Krauss-Varban (1994): Electron acceleration at nearly perpendicular collisionless shocks: 3. Downstream distributions

- Ref: J. Geophys. Res. 99(A2), 2537–2551 (1994); DOI 10.1029/93JA01643; peer-reviewed.
- Read: 2026-11-13, abstract; topics: spherical-shock-injection
- ABSTRACT ONLY (OpenAlex record). The full text was not obtained: the publisher host refused the request
  and no open copy was found. Nothing below goes beyond the abstract.

## Claim
Orbits of halo electrons in hybrid-simulation fields of a curved, nearly perpendicular shock give the
highest phase-space densities in the overshoot near theta_Bn = 90 deg; with artificial pitch-angle
scattering the energetic electrons spread to smaller theta_Bn and explain shock spike events far from
90 deg.

## Method
- Test-particle electrons in hybrid fields (fluid core electrons, kinetic ions); curved shock; runs with
  and without artificial pitch-angle scattering.

## Numbers to compare against
- "The shock curvature also leads to an accumulation of electrons close to 90 deg. Without pitch angle
  scattering, energization is only significant within a few (about 5 to 10 deg) degrees of the point of
  tangency."
- Enhancements up to about 4 orders of magnitude within several degrees of 90 deg; observed spike
  enhancements are 1–2 orders.
- With scattering: "much higher phase space densities at smaller theta_Bn than what a local
  one-dimensional mapping would predict".

## Assessment
- For this topic: a published statement that electrons energized near the tangent point are found at
  lower theta_Bn with more phase-space density than the local theory gives there. The transport is
  pitch-angle scattering and the electrons are downstream / in the overshoot, not backstreaming. The
  abstract does not say how far along the front they move.
