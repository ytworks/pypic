# Lowe & Burgess (2003): The properties and causes of rippling in quasi-perpendicular collisionless shock fronts

- Ref: Ann. Geophys. 21, 671–679 (2003); DOI 10.5194/angeo-21-671-2003; peer-reviewed.
- Read: 2026-11-13, abstract; topics: spherical-shock-injection
- ABSTRACT ONLY (OpenAlex record). The full text was not read. Nothing below goes beyond the abstract.

## Claim
The surface of a quasi-perpendicular, high Mach number shock is rippled, "with variations in the density
and all field components". The analysis suggests "the presence of a surface wave mode dominating the shock
normal magnetic field component of the ripples, as well as whistler waves excited by reflected ions."

## Method
- A two-dimensional hybrid simulation (particle ions, electron fluid).

## Numbers to compare against
- None in the abstract (no wavelength, speed or amplitude of the ripples).

## Assessment
- For this topic: background on ripples of the front. The ripples have a magnetic field component along
  the shock normal, so the field direction at the front varies along it. `burgess2006simulations` and
  `trotta2019electron` cite this work.
- Electrons are a fluid here: electron acceleration is not in the abstract. The abstract also says the
  process that generates the ripples "is poorly understood".
