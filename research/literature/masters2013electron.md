# Masters et al. (2013): Electron acceleration to relativistic energies at a strong quasi-parallel shock wave

- Ref: Nature Physics 9, 164-167; DOI 10.1038/nphys2541; arXiv:1301.1438 (13 authors: Masters, Stawarz,
  Fujimoto, Schwartz, Sergis, Thomsen, Retino, Hasegawa, ...). Peer reviewed. Read in the arXiv version
  (the submitted manuscript); the journal version was not compared.
- Read: 2026-11-14, full (arXiv v1, main text and figure captions); topics: qpar-electron-conditions

## Claim
At one crossing of Saturn's bow shock (2007-02-03) with an Alfven Mach number of about 100 and a
quasi-parallel geometry, Cassini measured locally accelerated electrons up to about 1 MeV. This contradicts
the solar-system result that electron acceleration is "effectively absent" at quasi-parallel shocks, and the
authors conclude that quasi-parallel shocks become effective electron accelerators at high Mach number.

## Method
- Cassini in situ: magnetometer, low-energy electrons (CAPS-ELS, below 18 keV in this paper's usage) and
  high-energy electrons (MIMI-LEMMS, above 18 keV), plasma waves.
- Shock parameters from Cassini data together with a solar wind propagation model; the sonic and fast
  Mach numbers use a "typical" temperature of the near-Saturn solar wind.

## Numbers to compare against
- Upstream field ~0.1 nT; M_A ~100; sonic and fast magnetosonic Mach numbers ~25.
- Geometry: before ~00:05 UT the spacecraft was not connected to the shock and the shock was
  quasi-perpendicular (theta_Bn ~60 deg); from ~00:05 UT it was connected and theta_Bn ~20 deg. The shock
  was crossed about one hour later.
- From ~00:05 UT a population with above-background intensities up to ~100 keV is seen upstream and
  downstream (Fig. 2d, 3a); the authors attribute this weak "background" mainly to electrons escaping from
  the magnetosphere. The shock-associated signal exceeds it and reaches ~1 MeV, the top of the detector range.
- The spectrum of the shock-accelerated electrons changes shape at ~100 keV (Fig. 4).
- SLAMS are present in the region with high-energy electrons (Fig. 2a). The authors: electrons at and above
  the energy of the spectral change "may have had gyroradii large enough to be efficiently scattered by SLAMS".
- "The high-energy electron profile of this shock encounter is not in agreement with diffusive shock
  acceleration theory, which predicts a constant downstream" intensity.

## Assessment
- One event. No pitch-angle information ("firm conclusions about directional particle fluxes or pitch angle
  distributions cannot be drawn"). The motion of the shock is not measured. M_A rests on a solar wind model.
- The highest energy is a lower limit: it sits at the upper end of the instrument.
- A seed population of up to ~100 keV on the connected field lines exists; the paper treats it as background,
  not as the source. Whether the MeV electrons come from thermal electrons or from this population is not
  decided by the data shown.
- Follow-ups: masters2016suprathermal (all crossings), masters2017situ (spectra); modelled with test
  particles in prescribed turbulence by guo2015acceleration (note on `research/spherical-shock-injection`).
