## Claim
At Saturn's bow shock, "significant local electron acceleration has been confirmed under quasi-parallel
magnetic conditions for the first time, contradicting the established magnetic dependence of electron
acceleration at solar system shocks."

## Method
- Cassini spacecraft observations of "an unusually strong solar system shock wave (Saturn's bow shock)".
  The Mach number and the shock angle are not in the abstract.

## Numbers to compare against
- Electron energies: "relativistic energies (~MeV), comparable to the highest energies ever attributed to
  shock-acceleration in the solar wind".

## Assessment
- For this topic: it speaks to the polar caps of a sphere. Established result at weaker shocks: electron
  acceleration is "effectively absent" under quasi-parallel conditions. Claim here: "at high-Mach
  numbers, like those of young supernova remnant shocks, quasi-parallel shocks become considerably more
  effective electron accelerators."
- It is one event, and the abstract names no mechanism. Whether the obliquity ordering of electron
  acceleration reverses with Mach number is therefore open in the abstract.
