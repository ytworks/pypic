# Masters et al. (2017): An in situ comparison of electron acceleration at collisionless shocks under differing upstream magnetic field orientations

- Ref: ApJ 843, 147; DOI 10.3847/1538-4357/aa76ea; arXiv:1705.11096 (9 authors: Masters, Sulaiman,
  Stawarz, Reville, Sergis, Fujimoto, Burgess, Coates, Dougherty). Peer reviewed. Read in the arXiv version;
  the volume and page were not re-checked against the journal.
- Read: 2026-11-14, full (arXiv v1); topics: qpar-electron-conditions

## Claim
Two Cassini crossings of Saturn's bow shock, one quasi-perpendicular (2008-03-08) and one quasi-parallel
(2007-02-03), both show electrons accelerated to at least MeV energies, with comparable intensities. The
quasi-parallel spectrum is harder above ~100 keV; the authors suggest that the break comes from an
energy-dependent interaction with SLAMS.

## Method
- Cassini magnetometer, CAPS and MIMI-LEMMS; power-law fits to the LEMMS electron spectra (Fig. 3).
- Discussion against diffusive shock acceleration (DSA) and against guo2015acceleration.

## Numbers to compare against
- Quasi-parallel crossing: upstream field ~0.1 nT, M_A ~100. Two power laws with a transition at ~100 keV,
  seen throughout ~00:30-01:20 UT; slopes of differential intensity about -4 +- 1 below and -2 +- 1 above
  100 keV (Fig. 3b; uncertainties are quoted to one significant figure).
- Quasi-perpendicular crossing (upstream field ~0.8 nT, M_A ~15, M_f ~10, for a flow of ~450 km/s and a
  shock at rest): a single power law, slope about -2.5 +- 0.5 (Fig. 3a). The index above 100 keV "is the
  same at the quasi-perpendicular and quasi-parallel shocks, to within the uncertainties".
- Typical M_A of the Cassini crossings ~15, with instances of ~5 and ~100; mostly quasi-perpendicular.
- SLAMS make the local shock angle highly variable (Fig. 2b). In the authors' estimate electrons below
  ~100 keV can be trapped in these structures (gyroradius below the SLAMS scale).
- The region with shock-reflected ions and the gyroradius of an MeV electron are both ~2000 km in their
  estimate; the shock moves toward the spacecraft at ~100 km/s.
- The 2007-02-03 event "remains the only confirmed case of in situ evidence for acceleration of electrons
  at a quasi-parallel shock" (state of 2017).

## Assessment
- Slopes carry errors of +-1: the break is clear, the values above it are weakly constrained.
- Two events; the comparison of absolute intensity does not control for the upstream seed population
  noted in masters2013electron.
- The paper states agreement with guo2015acceleration, where the turbulence amplitude is an input.
- Since then: relativistic electrons at Earth's quasi-parallel bow shock with foreshock transients
  (liu2019relativistic, raptis2025multi) and at a near-parallel interplanetary shock
  (jebaraj2024acceleration, note on `research/shock-alfvenic-turbulence`).
