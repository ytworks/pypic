# Morlino (2009): Cosmic-ray electron injection from the ionization of nuclei

- Ref: Phys. Rev. Lett. 103, 121102 (2009); DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.103.121102; arXiv:0908.3217 (v1,
  "accepted for publication in Physical Review Letters"); peer-reviewed.
- Read: 2026-11-13, abstract; topics: spherical-shock-injection
- ABSTRACT ONLY (arXiv abstract). Nothing below goes beyond the abstract.

## Claim
"the secondary electrons ejected from the ionization of heavy ions can be injected into the acceleration
process that occurs at supernova remnant shocks". The mechanism works because "ions are ionized during the
acceleration when they move already with relativistic speed, just like ejected electrons do".

## Method
- An estimate with "the abundances of heavy nuclei measured in cosmic rays at Earth". Whether it is analytic
  or numerical is not in the abstract.

## Numbers to compare against
- Electron/proton ratio at the source: ~1e-4, "big enough to account for the nonthermal synchrotron emission
  observed in young SNRs".
- "the ionization process can limit the maximum energy that heavy ions can reach": no number in the abstract.

## Assessment
- For this topic: background only. It is an alternative route to electron injection that needs no kinetic
  pre-acceleration at the shock front. It needs partially ionized heavy ions that are already accelerated.
- A pure electron-proton PIC run has no such channel. The work matters only when a claim is made that
  kinetic injection at the front is needed to explain the electrons of remnants: this route is a competitor.
