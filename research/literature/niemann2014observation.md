# Niemann et al. (2014): Observation of collisionless shocks in a large current-free laboratory plasma

- Ref: Geophys. Res. Lett. 41(21), 7413 (2014); DOI 10.1002/2014GL061820; peer-reviewed.
- Read: 2026-11-13, abstract; topics: spherical-shock-injection

  (HTTP 403). Nothing below goes beyond the abstract.

## Claim
First measurement of the formation and structure of a magnetised collisionless shock made by a
laser-driven magnetic piston in a current-free laboratory plasma. The shock leaves the piston.

## Method
- A high-energy laser makes a plasma plume in a large magnetised ambient plasma ("17 m x 0.6 m", large
  enough to support Alfven waves). Magnetic field measurements; comparison with 2-D hybrid simulations.

## Numbers to compare against
- Energy goes from the laser plume to the ambient ions by collisionless coupling "until a self-sustained
  M_A ~ 2 magnetosonic shock separates from the piston".
- The hybrid simulations "show Larmor coupling between the debris and ambient ions and the presence of
  reflected ions, which provide the dissipation".
- "The measured shock formation time confirms predictions from computational work." No value is given.

## Assessment
- For this topic: the laboratory case of a finite debris cloud. The shock that leaves the piston has
  M_A about 2. This is the same class of weak shock as in our runs.
- The abstract does not give the Mach number of the debris. Schaeffer et al. 2015 report M_A = 1.3-2.8
  for the pistons of the same platform; a strong shock was not reached there.
- The formation time and distance in gyro-units are in the full text, which was not read.
