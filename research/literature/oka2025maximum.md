# Oka, Makishima & Terasawa (2025): Maximum Energy of Particles in Plasmas

- Ref: ApJ 979, 161; DOI 10.3847/1538-4357/ad9916; arXiv:2412.00564. Peer reviewed. Read in the arXiv version.
- Read: 2026-11-14, abstract, the data table and the passages on Earth's and Saturn's bow shocks (arXiv
  HTML); the rest not read; topics: qpar-electron-conditions

## Claim
A systematic review over space, solar, astrophysical and laboratory plasmas: the observed maximum particle
energy often reaches the Hillas limit (system size times motional electric field). Exceptions exist, among
them electrons in solar flares and protons in planetary radiation belts.

## Method
- Compilation of flow speed V, field B and system size L per environment; Hillas energy from them; the
  observed maximum taken from the highest energy reported in the literature.

## Numbers to compare against
- Earth's bow shock: V 400-600 km/s, B 5-8 nT ("fast" wind), Hillas energy 190-760 keV; observed maximum
  300 keV for ions and for electrons.
- Saturn's bow shock: V 300-600 km/s, B 0.2-1.0 nT, Hillas energy 0.2-2 MeV; observed electron maximum
  700 keV; no ion value.

## Assessment
- For this topic: at both planets the observed electron maxima sit at the Hillas value within the quoted
  ranges, so a size limit (H1b) cannot be ruled out from the maxima alone.
- The observed maxima are the highest reported values, i.e. the Earth entry is a transient event
  (wilson2016relativistic, liu2019relativistic), and several are instrument ceilings.
- The Saturn row uses B 0.2-1.0 nT, not the ~0.1 nT of the 2007-02-03 crossing (masters2013electron).
- The Hillas limit is a necessary condition; the review does not address which shocks reach it.
