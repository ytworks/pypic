# Pais, Pfrommer, Ehlert & Pakmor (2018): The effect of cosmic-ray acceleration on supernova blast wave dynamics

- Ref: MNRAS 478, 5278-5295 (2018) (volume and pages from the Crossref record of the DOI);
  DOI 10.1093/mnras/sty1410; arXiv:1805.00128 (v1); peer-reviewed.
- Read: 2026-11-13, abstract; topics: spherical-shock-injection
- ABSTRACT ONLY (arXiv abstract). Nothing below goes beyond the abstract.

## Claim
With an obliquity-dependent acceleration of cosmic-ray (CR) ions, a Sedov-Taylor explosion in a homogeneous
magnetic field "generates an oblate ellipsoidal shock surface due to the slower propagating blast wave in the
direction of the magnetic field". The cause is the efficient CR production "in the quasi-parallel polar cap
regions, which softens the equation of state and increases the compressibility of the post-shock gas". The
solution stays self-similar; the ellipticity is constant in time.

## Method
- Moving-mesh code AREPO; a spherically expanding blast wave with a sub-grid, obliquity-dependent CR ion
  acceleration: efficient at quasi-parallel shocks, not at quasi-perpendicular ones.
- Field morphologies from homogeneous to turbulent, with varying coherence lengths.

## Numbers to compare against
- Maximum CR acceleration efficiency "of about 15 per cent at quasi-parallel shocks (as suggested by kinetic
  plasma simulations)" gives "an average efficiency of about 5 per cent, independent of the assumed magnetic
  coherence length".
- An effective ratio of specific heats as a function of the maximum efficiency: derived, values not in the
  abstract.

## Assessment
- For this topic: the same geometry at the fluid scale, a spherical blast wave in a uniform field with
  theta_Bn from 0 at the poles to 90 deg at the equator. It concerns ions only. The obliquity dependence is
  put in from plane-shock kinetic results; it is not tested on a curved front.
- It shows what an injection law against theta_Bn is used for downstream of kinetic work. An electron law of
  this kind for a sphere is what this topic could supply; the abstract does not treat electrons.
