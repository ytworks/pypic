# Pulupa & Bale (2008): Structure on interplanetary shock fronts: Type II radio burst source regions

- Ref: Astrophys. J. 676, 1330–1337 (2008); DOI 10.1086/526405; peer-reviewed. Read from the publisher's
  open PDF.
- Read: 2026-11-13, skimmed (Sect. 1 and Sect. 5–7 read; the data sections in outline);
  topics: spherical-shock-injection

## Claim
In situ type II source regions at interplanetary shocks are foreshock regions upstream of structure
("bays") on the shock front, with scales of tens of Mm, in which a field line can be connected to the
shock in both directions.
- Question: what is the scale of the shock-front structure that lets a straight upstream field line touch
  an interplanetary shock?
- Idea: fit the velocity dispersion of the foreshock electron beams seen by Wind to get the distance to
  the tangent point.

## Method
- Observation (Wind, 1996–2002): 377 shocks, 125 with possible upstream Langmuir activity, in situ type II
  regions at eight shocks; three analysed (1997 May 15, 1998 Aug 26/28, 2000 Feb 11).
- Fit d_par = v_e (t_onset - t_0) (Eq. 4) for the parallel distance to the source.

## Numbers to compare against
- Table 3: d_perp 15–28 Mm (2.3–4.3 R_E), d_par 78–151 Mm (12.2–32.6 R_E); theta_Bn > 80 deg at the
  spacecraft.
- Sect. 1: "The efficiency of fast Fermi acceleration at curved shocks peaks when the IMF lines are nearly
  tangent to the shock (Krauss-Varban et al. 1989; Krauss-Varban & Burgess 1991) ... a straight upstream IMF
  line has no tangent point to a shock unless curvature is present on the shock front".
- Sect. 1: "Cairns (1986) proposed a time-of-flight mechanism for type II emission generated by a curved IP
  shock analogous to the Filbert & Kellogg (1979) mechanism".
- Sect. 7: "The 1997 May 15 and 1998 August 28 shocks have evidence for foreshock structure in both the
  parallel and antiparallel directions, suggesting the presence of a bay in which electron beams can be
  mirrored and accelerated".

## Assessment
- Shown: observed scale of front structure; evidence for double connection at two of three shocks.
- Stated limits: one spacecraft cannot separate a curved field from a curved front (citing Erdős & Balogh
  1994; Decker 1990 for ions at a rippled shock); the cause of the structure is not known.
- For this topic: an observational source for "field line connected to the shock at two points" and a
  pointer to the time-of-flight literature (Filbert & Kellogg 1979; Cairns 1986), which were not obtained.
  It has no model of electron energies.
