# Pulupa, Bale, Opitz, Fedorov, Lin & Sauvaud (2012): STEREO measurements of electron acceleration beyond fast Fermi at the bow shock

- Ref: arXiv:1202.3678 (read); the manuscript header names J. Geophys. Res.; volume, pages and DOI were not
  verified, and the author list is as on the arXiv copy.
- Read: 2026-11-13, skimmed (abstract, Sect. 1, Sect. 5); topics: spherical-shock-injection

## Claim
Foreshock electron spectra measured by STEREO up to several tens of keV exceed what fast Fermi reflection
of the measured solar wind distribution gives (Liouville mapping); another process is needed above a few
hundred eV to a few keV.

## Method
- Observation (STEREO SWEA and STE) against the Liouville-mapped fast Fermi prediction, for a crossing of
  the foreshock edge and for a point deeper in the foreshock.

## Numbers to compare against
- Foreshock edge (Sect. 5.1, Fig. 4): cutoff speed v_c about 13000 km/s, v_HT about 1500 km/s; power law
  Gamma = 2.04 with roll-off E_0 = 5.5 keV.
- Deeper foreshock (Sect. 5.3): v_c about 4250 km/s, v_HT about 500 km/s; departure from fast Fermi starts
  at the 650 eV bin.
- Scales: r_Li = 74 km, c/omega_pi = 123 km; the electron gyroradius reaches them at 87 keV and 32 keV.
- Sect. 5.4: a strong bump-on-tail from the halo alone only "in a narrow region where theta_bn is very
  close to 90 deg"; the strahl widens the range.

## Assessment
- Sect. 5.2 is a compact review of candidates: downstream leakage (Gosling et al. 1989), SDA with multiple
  encounters (Ball & Melrose 2001), scattering (Vandas 2001), ripples with a field line connected "to
  multiple points on the surface of a rippled shock" (Burgess 2006; observed at CME-driven shocks, Bale et
  al. 1999, Pulupa & Bale 2008), whistlers (Oka et al. 2006; Amano & Hoshino 2010), Guo & Giacalone 2010,
  and the trapped and leaked classes of Savoini et al. 2010.
- For this topic: an observational statement that single-encounter fast Fermi at the curved bow shock
  under-predicts the energetic electrons; no new mechanism is shown.
