# Qin, Wang & Kong (2026): Electron Acceleration Efficiency at Earth's Bow Shock: Statistical Results

- Ref: ApJ 1005, 153; DOI 10.3847/1538-4357/ae71bc. Peer reviewed.
- Read: 2026-11-14, abstract only (full text not obtained); topics: qpar-electron-conditions

## Claim
In 1492 bow shock events the electron acceleration efficiency varies widely with shock conditions.
Quasi-perpendicular shocks are generally more efficient than quasi-parallel ones, which the authors read as
shock drift acceleration being dominant.

## Method
- Statistical study of selected bow shock crossings; efficiency defined as eta = n_dn / n_am, the ratio of
  the downstream to the ambient electron number density (energy range not given in the abstract).
- Spectra before and after the shock fitted with double power laws.

## Numbers to compare against
- 1492 events; eta from 0 to 800.
- High-efficiency events also cluster near the subsolar point, about 1.4 times the efficiency of the
  quasi-perpendicular region.
- Efficiency rises with compression ratio, with a dependence that changes with shock normal angle; flow
  speed, Mach number and plasma beta also matter.
- Ambient spectra are steeper at low than at high energy; accelerated spectra show the opposite.

## Assessment
- Abstract only: the energy range, the instrument and the quasi-parallel numbers are not known to us.
- The abstract does not say which geometry the subsolar high-efficiency events have; position and
  geometry are not separated there.
- Suprathermal electrons, not relativistic ones: it bears on the seed population (H4), not on > 100 keV.
- Related statistics at Earth and Venus exist from the same period (DOI 10.3847/2041-8213/ae7de4,
  10.1029/2026JA035464, 10.3847/1538-4357/ac8157); screened by title only.
