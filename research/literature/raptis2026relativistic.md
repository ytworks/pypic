# Raptis, Turner, Caprioli, Szalay, Clark & Haggerty (2026): Relativistic electron acceleration at the bow shock of Jupiter and beyond

- Ref: Nature 654, 47-51; DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10473-z. Peer reviewed; open access.
- Read: 2026-11-14, abstract, the event section, the scaling section and the stated assumptions (journal
  HTML); methods skimmed; topics: qpar-electron-conditions

## Claim
Juno measured electrons of at least 1 MeV upstream of Jupiter's bow shock, inside a large foreshock
transient. With Earth and Saturn, the authors propose a scaling for the Hillas limit: the size of the
transient follows from the size of the shock system, and the size sets the maximum electron energy.

## Method
- Juno crossing of the duskward flank of the bow shock, 2023-10-01 ~18:05 UTC; fields and energetic
  electrons.
- Empirical relation between the stand-off distance S of the shock and the size L of the acceleration
  region (Fig. 3a); maximum energy from equating the Bohm diffusion length (D ~ v r_g / 3) to L (Fig. 3b).
- Extrapolation to the protostellar jet HH 211, SN 1987A and SN 1006.

## Numbers to compare against
- >= 1 MeV electrons at Jupiter; the spectrum softens from about E^-1.5 to E^-2 above ~1 MeV.
- E_max ~ 3 q B V_sh L (Bohm, size-limited).
- The scaling is shown consistent with the observed maxima at Earth, Saturn and Jupiter (Fig. 3b).
- Predictions: MeV at planets, about tens of GeV for protostellar jets, about tens of TeV for supernova
  remnants; acceleration regions of 1e8-1e10 km for the extrapolated systems.
- Stated assumptions: "Bohm diffusion well describes the environment (that is, the magnetic fluctuations
  are stronger than the background field, dB/B0 >> 1) and that sufficient time is available".

## Assessment
- For this topic this is the published form of the size-limit hypothesis (H1b): three planets on one
  line, with Bohm scattering and enough time assumed, not measured.
- The Jupiter electrons are inside a transient; the paper does not show relativistic electrons at an
  unperturbed quasi-parallel shock.
- The diffusion coefficient is an assumption. dB/B0 >> 1 does not hold in the measured precursor of the
  PSP shock (~10 %, jebaraj2026wave), where relativistic electrons are nevertheless seen.
- A relation fitted to three systems, extended over many orders of magnitude; the prediction interval of
  the fit is shown in Fig. 3a, the uncertainty of the Bohm assumption is not propagated.
