# Reynoso, Hughes & Moffett (2013): On the radio polarization signature of efficient and inefficient particle acceleration in supernova remnant SN 1006

- Ref: Astronomical Journal (accepted, per arXiv comment); DOI 10.1088/0004-6256/145/4/104 (the DOI
  encodes vol. 145, no. 4, art. 104; not otherwise checked); arXiv:1302.4678v1. Peer-reviewed article.
- Read: 2026-11-12, full; topics: spherical-shock-injection

## Claim
In SN 1006 the bright NE and SW lobes are weakly polarised (disordered field), while the faint SE rim
is polarised close to the theoretical maximum with the field tangent to the shock and parallel to the
Galactic plane. The ambient field therefore runs NE-SW, the lobes are polar caps, and acceleration with
turbulence generation is efficient at quasi-parallel and inefficient at quasi-perpendicular shocks.

## Method
Observation. VLA (CnD) plus ATCA (6B, 6A, 750C) full-polarisation continuum at 20 cm (bands near
1370/1384 and 1665/1704 MHz), taken in 2003; restored beam 10 arcsec; sigma_Q = sigma_U = 0.085
mJy/beam; polarised intensity blanked below 2 sigma. Rotation measure from the two bands; one mean
value applied to the whole remnant; vectors rotated by 90 deg to give the projected field. Field angles
are analysed relative to the local radial direction and to the Galactic plane (position angle 60 deg).
No simulation: m_i/m_e, Mach numbers, beta do not apply; shock speed and density: not stated.

## Numbers to compare against
- Polarisation fraction 0.17 (17 +- 7 %) in the lobes; 0.6 +- 0.2 on the SE arc (azimuth 113-180
  deg, radius 880 arcsec); maximum for a uniform field (alpha+1)/(alpha+5/3) = 70.5 % for alpha = 0.6
  (Sects. 3.1, 4; Fig. 4).
- With Burn (1966), 17 % means that only 24 % of the magnetic energy is in an ordered component
  (Sect. 4).
- Rotation measure 12 rad/m^2 (sigma 20); NE 12, SW 14.5 (Sect. 2, Fig. 2).
- Field angle from the radial direction: NE peak 9 deg (sigma 20), SW -4 deg (sigma 25): radial.
  SE peak 112 deg on a 0-180 deg scale (sigma 18): tangential. Outermost SW rim (860-920 arcsec):
  peak 88 deg (sigma 30): tangential (Sect. 3.2, Figs. 7-8).
- Position angles: a narrow component at 58 deg beside a broad one at 45 deg (Fig. 9); in the lobes
  47 % of the vectors cluster at 58 deg (Fig. 10); SE sector 54 deg with sigma 10 deg (Sect. 4).
- Distance 2.18 +- 0.08 kpc, diameter about 30 arcmin (Sect. 1); flux 17 Jy, polarised 2 Jy (Sect. 2).
- Quoted from Cassam-Chenai et al. (2008): at the SE rim less than 10 % of the shock energy goes into
  accelerated particles and a 3 muG field compressed by 4 suffices (no amplification); the gap between
  blast wave and contact discontinuity is largest in the SE and shrinks towards the lobes (Sects. 1, 4).

## Assessment
- Shown by observation: the polarisation contrast between lobes and SE rim, and a fixed-angle field
  component along the Galactic plane direction.
- Inferred: that the fixed-angle component is the ambient field; that low polarisation means
  turbulence driven by accelerated particles; the dependence of efficiency on theta_Bn follows from
  this geometry. The data give projected directions only, no inclination to the line of sight.
- The statement is about "particle acceleration" and turbulence in general: electron injection is not
  separated from acceleration or from field amplification, and no injection law is fitted.
- Left open by the authors: the patchy mix of radial and fixed-angle vectors inside the lobes "remains
  to be understood"; the tangential field at the SW outer edge needs more sensitive data.
- Limits: uniform rotation measure assumed (half the value in the SE would turn the vectors by 10
  deg); short spacings missing (17 Jy against 18.68 Jy with single dish); the SE error is an RMS
  scatter, not an error of the mean.
- Alternatives discussed: the equatorial-belt reading (Fulbright & Reynolds 1990; Petruk et al. 2009;
  Schneiter et al. 2010), with faster acceleration but lower injection at perpendicular shocks
  (Jokipii 1987; Ellison et al. 1995), is rejected; Rayleigh-Taylor fingers (Jun & Norman 1996)
  explain the radial field of the lobes only in part; Dolginov et al. (2004) is used for the rest.
- Slip: Sect. 3.1 calls the bright lobes "SE and NW" (NE and SW are meant). The field-gradient model
  is credited to Petruk et al. (2011), not checked; bocchino2011constraints is not cited.
