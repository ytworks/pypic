# Rothenflug, Ballet, Dubner, Giacani, Decourchelle & Ferrando (2004): Geometry of the non-thermal emission in SN 1006: Azimuthal variations of cosmic-ray acceleration

- Ref: Astron. Astrophys. 425, 121–131 (2004); DOI 10.1051/0004-6361:20047104; peer-reviewed.
- Read: 2026-11-13, abstract; topics: spherical-shock-injection
- ABSTRACT ONLY (OpenAlex record). The full text was not read. Nothing below goes beyond the abstract.

## Claim
The bright non-thermal limbs of SN 1006 are polar caps: "The very low brightness above 2 keV in the
interior indicates that the bright non-thermal limbs are polar caps rather than an equator. This implies
that the ambient magnetic field runs southwest to northeast, along the Galactic plane."

## Method
- A full X-ray map from all XMM-Newton observations; a combined VLA/Parkes radio map fixes the spectrum at
  low energy.
- Spectra are fitted with synchrotron emission from a cut-off power-law electron distribution plus a
  thermal component. Radial and azimuthal profiles of the cut-off frequency are given.

## Numbers to compare against
- No values in the abstract. Trends only: the cut-off frequency "decreases steeply with radius towards the
  center and with position angle away from the maximum emission".
- "The maximum energy reached by accelerated particles, as well as their number, must be higher at the
  bright limbs than elsewhere."

## Assessment
- For this topic: an observational argument that in SN 1006 both the number and the maximum energy of the
  electrons are highest at the polar caps, where the ambient field is along the shock normal. The
  abstract says this "implies interesting constraints for acceleration at perpendicular shocks".
- The data are X-ray synchrotron electrons (the highest energies), not the injection step. The abstract
  names no mechanism; it says the data agree with a model "in which the magnetic field is amplified where
  acceleration is efficient".
