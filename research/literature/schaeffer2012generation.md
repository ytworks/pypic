## Claim
Experiments on the Trident laser show the elements of a laser-driven magnetised shock: a magnetic piston
that couples laser energy to a background plasma, and a shock precursor. Fast ions decouple; slow ions
couple.

## Method
- Trident laser (Los Alamos); laser-ablated plasma in a magnetised background plasma.
- 2-D hybrid simulations in support.

## Numbers to compare against
- "evidence of decoupling between a laser-driven fast ion population and a background plasma, in contrast
  to the coupling of laser-ablated slow ions with background ions through the magnetic piston".
- The simulations show "the formation of a magnetic and density compression pulses consistent with a
  collisionless shock, and the decoupling of the fast ions".
- The abstract gives no numbers.

## Assessment
- For this topic: in one expansion the fast debris ions decouple and the slow, dense ones drive the
  piston. This supports a slow dense debris core as the driver; the fast part of a debris distribution
  is lost to the coupling. The reading is ours.
- Only a precursor is claimed, not a strong shock.
