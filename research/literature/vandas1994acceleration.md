# Vandas (1994): Acceleration of electrons at a curved shock

- Ref: Astrophys. J. Suppl. 90, 583–588 (1994) (IAU Colloq. 142); DOI 10.1086/191878; peer-reviewed
  proceedings issue of a journal. Read from the copy at cambridge.org (DOI 10.1017/S0252921100077848).
- Read: 2026-11-13, full; topics: spherical-shock-injection

## Claim
Curvature of the shock shortens the interaction of a reflected electron and so lowers the gain of shock
drift acceleration. The mean gain of reflected electrons depends on R_c/d (radius of curvature over shock
thickness): about 1/4 of the plane-shock gain for the bow shock, about 3/4 for interplanetary shocks.
- Question (Sect. 1): how much does curvature limit the gain, given that the gain is very sensitive to
  theta_Bn near 90 deg?
- Idea: treat one interaction analytically in the adiabatic (guiding-centre) approximation, with a curved
  shock written as a plane shock whose normal field grows along the front, B_n = B_1 z / R_c (after
  Krauss-Varban & Burgess 1991, named here as the first treatment of a curved shock for electrons).

## Method
- Analytic / numerical guiding-centre orbits of suprathermal electrons; no cross-shock potential; no
  scattering; fields prescribed (not self-consistent).
- Plane shock of thickness d with three profiles (A exponential, B linear, C); curved shock cylindrical
  (2-D) and spherical (3-D); the 3-D case reduces to the 2-D one (Sect. 3.3).
- Parameters: B_1 = 10 nT, V_1 = 400 (800) km/s, field jump nu = 3, R_c = 40 R_E, d = 100 km,
  E_i = 1 (10) keV, alpha_i = 80 deg, theta_Bn,i = 88.9 / 89.1 deg. No m_i/m_e, M_A or beta enters.

## Numbers to compare against
- Plane shock: E_f = E_i + 2 m V_B^2 + 2 m V_B v_i cos alpha_i (Eq. 8); maximum gain 10 x for nu = 3,
  18 x for nu = 5.
- Curved shock (Eqs. 10–12): energy against time follows the plane-shock curve but the interaction ends
  much earlier for reflected electrons (Figs. 3, 4).
- Mean gain ratio eta of reflected electrons against R_c/d (Fig. 7), averaged over alpha_i and over
  theta_Bn,i in 88–90 deg: about 1/4 for R_c/d of 1e2–1e3 (bow shock), about 3/4 for 1e4–1e5
  (interplanetary shocks).
- Fig. 6: E_f falls from about 6.3 keV at d = 0 to about 3 keV at d = 1000 km (plane value about 6.7 keV).
- Transmitted electrons gain about as at a plane shock (Fig. 5).
- A new class "reflected through the nose": small alpha_i, theta_Bn,i within 1–2 deg of 90 deg; gain only
  slightly above the transmitted one. Reflected electrons then exist at all theta_Bn,i.

## Assessment
- Shown: a closed description of single-encounter SDA at a curved front in the adiabatic limit, with R_c/d
  as the controlling ratio.
- Stated limits: adiabatic, non-relativistic, one encounter, no potential, no scattering.
- Open (Sect. 4): "If a process (like scattering) holds electrons longer in the shock layer, they could get
  considerably higher energies, mainly for shocks with a large extent and thickness (application to SNR?—
  but this should need a relativistic generalization)."
- For this topic: the dependence on R_c/d and the electron that enters on one side of the tangent point and
  leaves on the other are published here. The front is a standing curved shock; expansion or deceleration
  of the front is not in the model.
- Later papers of the series (Vandas 1995a,b; Vandas 2001, J. Geophys. Res.) were reached as abstracts only.
