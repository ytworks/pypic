# Völk, Berezhko & Ksenofontov (2003): Variation of cosmic ray injection across supernova shocks

- Ref: Astron. Astrophys. 409, 563–571 (2003); DOI 10.1051/0004-6361:20031082; arXiv:astro-ph/0306016;
  peer-reviewed.
- Read: 2026-11-13, abstract; topics: spherical-shock-injection
- ABSTRACT ONLY (OpenAlex record). The full text was not read. Nothing below goes beyond the abstract.

## Claim
The injection of suprathermal protons into diffusive shock acceleration varies strongly over the surface
of a remnant shock. "The injection rate depends strongly on the shock obliquity and diminishes as the
angle between the ambient field and the shock normal increases. Therefore efficient particle injection
... arises only in relatively small regions near the 'poles'".

## Method
- Geometry: "the shock can be approximated as spherical in a uniform large-scale magnetic field" (the case
  of SN 1006).
- The sizes of the polar regions depend on the random background field and on the Alfvén wave turbulence
  from the cosmic-ray streaming instability. The injection model itself is not in the abstract.

## Numbers to compare against
- Efficiently injecting part of the shock surface: about 20 % for SN 1006 and for Tycho's remnant; between
  10 and 20 % for Cas A.
- The cosmic-ray production rate computed in spherical symmetry is to be renormalised by this factor.

## Assessment
- For this topic: the same global geometry as ours (a sphere in a uniform field), and a clear statement
  of the polar-cap picture. It is about protons and about injection into diffusive acceleration;
  electrons are not in the abstract.
- The abstract treats each surface element by its local obliquity. Transport of particles along the front
  between obliquities is not in the abstract.
