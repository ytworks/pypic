# Wilson et al. (2025): Large-amplitude Whistler Precursors and >MeV Particles Observed at a Weak Interplanetary Shock by Parker Solar Probe

- Ref: ApJ 987, 31; DOI 10.3847/1538-4357/add6a8 (12 authors: Wilson, Mitchell, Szabo, Jebaraj, Stevens,
  Malaspina, Berland, Kouloumvakos, ...). Peer reviewed.
- Read: 2026-11-14, abstract only (full text not obtained); topics: qpar-electron-conditions

## Claim
A weak interplanetary shock close to the Sun had ~MeV protons and synchrotron radiation from ~MeV
electrons upstream, which standard shock acceleration does not predict for such a shock. Its whistler
precursor was two orders of magnitude larger in amplitude than any seen before.

## Method
- PSP fields and particles at the shock of 2024-09-29, ~17 solar radii from the Sun.

## Numbers to compare against
- M_f ~1.2, theta_Bn ~50 deg, shock normal speed ~465 km/s.
- Precursor: magnetic amplitude > 700 nT peak to peak, electric field > 2000 mV/m, Poynting flux
  > 230 mW/m^2.
- Normalized wave parameters (e.g. frequency) agree with precursors near 1 au. The authors conclude that
  the precursor cannot give a larger fraction of energetic particles than at 1 au, but that the larger
  amplitudes "would allow for higher maximum energies".

## Assessment
- A "yes" at a shock that is weak by every local measure: local Mach number does not decide it.
- theta_Bn ~50 deg: oblique, not quasi-parallel; outside the cases of this topic, but a direct challenge
  to an M_A threshold (H2).
- The ~MeV electrons are inferred from synchrotron radiation, not measured in situ, per the abstract.
- Abstract only: whether the particles were accelerated locally or earlier (H7) is not known to us; the
  "inverse velocity arrival" of the protons suggests arrival from a source, not a local population.
