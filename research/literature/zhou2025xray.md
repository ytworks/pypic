# Zhou, Slane, Prokhorov, Vink, Ferrazzoli et al. (2025): X-ray polarization in SN 1006 southwest shows spatial variations and differences with the radio band

- Ref: ApJ 986, 210 (2025) (volume and article number from the Crossref record of the DOI);
  DOI 10.3847/1538-4357/add532; arXiv:2505.09712 (v1, "Accepted for publication in ApJ"); peer-reviewed.
- Read: 2026-11-13, abstract; topics: spherical-shock-injection
- ABSTRACT ONLY (arXiv abstract). Nothing below goes beyond the abstract.

## Claim
The X-ray polarization degree (PD) varies along the south-western shell of SN 1006. It is lower in the west,
where the shell "has been proposed to be interacting with an interstellar cloud". The fields traced by X-ray
polarization are nearly radial; those traced by radio polarization "tend to follow a direction parallel to the
Galactic plane".

## Method
- IXPE X-ray polarimetry of SN 1006 SW; comparison with MeerKAT radio polarization.

## Numbers to compare against
- Shell average, 2-4 keV: PD = 21.6% +/- 4.5%, polarization angle = -48 deg +/- 5 deg, "similar to those in
  the northeastern shell".
- Peak PD = 40% +/- 8% in the south; PD <~ 27% (99% upper limit) in the west.
- Scale of the magnetic turbulence from the high PD in the south: <~ 0.1 pc.

## Assessment
- For this topic: background only. The abstract reads the X-ray polarization as "freshly amplified magnetic
  fields from small-scale structures in the immediate postshock region" and the radio as "more extended
  regions influenced by the pre-existing ambient magnetic fields". So the X-ray field direction is not the
  ambient field direction at the bright limbs.
- The abstract says nothing on electron injection against theta_Bn. It supports only the statement that the
  field right behind this shock is turbulent and amplified, and that this depends on the pre-shock density.
