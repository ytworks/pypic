# Review — purpose stage (2026-11-12)

This review was made by the research reviewer (`.claude/agents/research-reviewer.md`, run as a

## Summary

The README asks three things about a spherical shock that expands into a uniform field:
- which mechanisms inject electrons;
- where on the front each one acts, as a function of the local θ_Bn, M_A and curvature;
- which channels exist only because the front is curved.

The claimed novelty, not yet verified, is to attribute injection over the whole θ_Bn range on one curved 3-D
full-PIC shock, and to find channels that only a curved front has. As written this is a programme, not a
question. No single publishable result can be named yet; that is the first major issue.

## Elements

- **Background:** present. One statement is wrong as worded: it says the known mechanisms are "framed for planar
  shocks", but amano2022theory Sect. 4.5 (Eq. 22) already limits stochastic shock drift acceleration (SSDA) by
  the curvature of the bow shock.
- **Research question:** missing. The draft lists eight classes of mechanism plus "unknown".
- **Prior work:** missing. None of the four notes deals with a curved front.
- **Idea:** weak. The README itself says the obstacle may be computing time; by the stance that is effort, not an idea.
- **Related work and novelty:** missing; the survey has not been done.
- **Evidence:** missing. The criteria and null tests are open, and the README marks them so correctly.
- **Publication:** missing.

## Major issues

1. **Scope.** "Every mechanism, including unknown ones" is not one question small enough to answer (stance). The
   draft defines an "unknown mechanism" as gain that no channel explains. That is the residual of a decomposition,
   so it measures how the channels were defined (standard 5), not a new mechanism. To resolve:
   - narrow to one agent and one parameter;
   - treat unexplained gain as a question only once it has a positive signature and a control run with it
     switched off (hoshino2015angular).
2. **The direct predecessors are missing.** These were found by web search and have not been read:
   - Krauss-Varban & Burgess 1991: in a curved-shock model, electrons entering near θ_Bn = 90° drift and leave at
     a lower θ_Bn. Known here only from a citation in Burgess, astro-ph/0610714.
   - Burgess, astro-ph/0610714: the effect of fluctuations of the shock surface on electron acceleration.
   - Guo & Giacalone 2010, ApJ 715, 406: the local θ_Bn varies along the front because field lines meander.
   - Caprioli & Spitkovsky 2014, ApJ 783, 91: hybrid simulations of how ion injection depends on θ_Bn, which
     sets the quasi-parallel structure.

   One search found no PIC study of electron injection against θ_Bn on a single curved shock; one search is not a
   survey. Read these before any run, because they decide what a "curvature-only" channel can still be.
3. **At m_i/m_e = 16–25, the mass ratio sets the θ_Bn map.** The reviewer's evaluation at the engine's β_e = 0.32:
   - amano2010critical Eq. 2 gives M_A,inj ≈ 1.1 cos θ_Bn at m_i/m_e = 16, which M_A ≈ 2.6 meets at every
     latitude. At m_i/m_e = 1836 it gives ≈ 12 cos θ_Bn, met only for θ_Bn ≳ 78°.
   - The surfing threshold (hoshino2002nonthermal Eq. 7) is ≈ 3–4 at m_i/m_e = 16, against 43–75 at the real mass ratio.
   - amano2022theory Eq. 21 has p*/p₁ ∝ m_i/m_e.

   These thresholds scale with different powers of the mass ratio. So "which mechanism, and where" is an artefact
   of the mass ratio unless the answer is stated as a scaling and tested at two or more mass ratios.
4. **The driver does not yet give the θ_Bn range.** From `examples/blast_wave/README.md`:
   - the front is measured only in the plane through the centre across B₀;
   - along B₀ the debris spreads further (rms distance 36.8 against 14.8 d_e);
   - the front decays (the ring mean of B_z falls from 2.6 to 1.5 B₀);
   - M_A ≈ 2.6 from the front speed (0.1627c over v_A = 0.0625c);
   - the front reaches R ≈ 19 d_i in about 7/Ω_ci.

   Consequences:
   - θ_Bn must come from the measured front normal, not from the polar angle.
   - The local M_A varies together with θ_Bn and with time, so "planar controls at matched local M_A" need a
     definition first.
   - Whether a quasi-parallel shock exists near the poles at this size and run time is not shown (not checked).
5. **Curvature needs its controlling number.** Curvature enters as r_g/R, or through the change of θ_Bn along the
   drift path (amano2022theory Eq. 22). With L_s ≈ M_A d_i (amano2022theory Sect. 2.5), these runs have R/L_s ≈ 7.
   Rough estimates: ~10² for Earth's bow shock and ≳ 10⁹ for supernova remnants. A channel that disappears as
   R/L_s → ∞ says nothing about supernova remnants, which are the Background's motivation. To resolve:
   - scan R/L_s (`gate` and `rm12` exist);
   - name the target system;
   - show that the planar limit is recovered.
6. **The planned attribution cannot separate the channels it lists.**
   - Surfing and shock drift acceleration both draw on the motional field along the same drift direction
     (hoshino2002nonthermal Sect. 3), so a split into parallel and drift work puts them in one bin.
   - The guiding-centre split is undefined wherever μ is broken.
   - Classify instead by whether μ is conserved and whether the electron is trapped in an electrostatic solitary
     wave (hoshino2002nonthermal Figs. 3–4).
   - In the ideal axisymmetric picture, the motional E and the ∇B and curvature drifts are azimuthal, that is, at
     constant θ_Bn. Electrons change latitude only by moving parallel to the draped downstream field. The Idea
     should state this minimal model before it assumes transport from quasi-perpendicular to quasi-parallel regions.

## Minor issues

- **Superluminal band.** The region where cos θ_Bn < V_sh/c covers a fraction V_sh/c of the sphere: ≈ 16 % at
  0.16c, against ≈ 1 % at 3000 km/s. Theory built on the de Hoffmann–Teller frame fails there (amano2022theory
  Sects. 2.6, 4.4). Flag the band in every analysis.
- **Resolution and filter** (estimate, not checked). The Buneman wavelength 2πu/ω_pe with u ≈ 2V_sh is about
  4 cells at Δx = 0.5 d_e, and the 4-pass filter may damp it strongly. If so, a missing surfing signal would be
  numerical. A control run with finer resolution or the filter changed is needed before any null result.
- **First-order Fermi (diffusive) acceleration cannot develop at this size.** r_g > L_s needs γβ ≳ 2.6, and the
  diffusion lengths approach R. Define "injected" by a threshold (r_g > L_s, or p*), not by seeing that
  acceleration in the run.
- **Wakefield.** As studied in Hoshino 2008 (on the reading list), it needs relativistic magnetised shocks. Argue
  it in or out of scope from its condition.
- **Statistics.** θ_Bn < 30° covers only 13 % of the front. At 8 particles per cell, estimate the tail counts per
  θ_Bn band against the one-count level before choosing the set-up.
- **Tracked electrons.** The tracked particles are a set fixed at the start (OUT-D3). Selecting injected electrons
  in a rerun needs orbits that repeat, but FP64 blast-wave runs with the same seed differ by up to 4e-5. Check
  whether orbits repeat, or use large id-strided sets.
- Item 7 (the shape of the front) is a structure, not a mechanism.

## Questions

1. Which system and observable is the target, and what is R/L_s there?
2. What is "injected", per θ_Bn band, in numbers?
3. Does a shock form at θ_Bn < 45° in this driver, and by what criterion?
4. Which mechanism can be switched off in a control run?

## Shortest path to certainty

Proposed question: on one curved front, at which θ_Bn does injection switch on? Does that angle move with m_i/m_e
and β_e as amano2010critical Eq. 2 predicts, or as amano2022theory Eq. 21 predicts? Each paper has its own
definition of injection. The reviewer's reading of the notes gives cos θ_c ∝ (m_i/m_e)^(-1/2) β_e^(-1/2) for
Eq. 2 and ∝ (m_i/m_e)^(-1) for Eq. 21; check both against the papers.

The idea is that the sphere gives the whole θ_Bn axis in one run. Both papers list a kinetic test as open.

0. Do the survey in issue 2, and check that no planar scan over θ_Bn has already done this test.
1. Without new runs, measure the front normal, θ_Bn and M_A(θ, t) off the equator in the existing blast-wave
   outputs. Whether those outputs contain enough to do this was not checked.
2. Choose M_A, β_e and V_sh/c so that both predicted θ_c lie in the subluminal range at m_i/m_e = 16 and 64. The
   predicted ratios of cos θ_c are 2 (Eq. 2) and 4 (Eq. 21). Run two seeds, add a control with the resolution or
   filter changed, and fix the criteria before the runs. If no such choice exists, test one of the two theories.
3. Make one run at a larger R/L_s. If θ_c does not move, the result is free of curvature; if it moves, compare
   with amano2022theory Eq. 22.
