## Findings

### A. The verdict (asks 1 and 6)

**1. The strongest case for "candidate" does not survive.** (`exploratory-results.md` Sects. 1, 5, 6, 7; protocol Sects. 1, 7, 8.)

- **Returns beyond the mirror baseline.**
  - They are unregistered, all-energy and thermal (median gain 0.02–0.04, which is 2–4 T_e). The protocol defines a residual at γ ≥ 1.5, so they are not one.
  - They sit where the numerical floor is failed by a wide margin (finding 6).
  - Thermal escape along the field reproduces their size to order of magnitude with no free parameter (finding 13).
- **Absent mirror beam.**
  - It is a deficit, and a hypothesis needs a positive signature.
  - It lies at θ_Bn ≳ 70°, where the protocol's own parameter ε is of order one and the baseline energy is "known only as a range" (finding 8).
  - `vandas1994acceleration` and R1 major issue 1 predicted this failure of the baseline.
- **K1 electrons.**
  - There is 1 in L and 2 in M against 100 required.
  - In `census.json` the L electron is flagged "beyond the debris: 0", so it would fail C3 (ρ_P > ρ_debris), and no dump of the valid domain holds a K1 electron.
  - The M electrons precede the first valid front.
- **Early energetic electrons that scale with the field.**
  - They are made before any valid front, so C3 fails by construction.
  - The gain is perpendicular and occurs at the piston stage.
  - The effect is known in the laser-plasma literature (finding 15).
- **Front history.** The stall near the equal-mass radius and the decay are the expected piston-to-blast-wave transition. Nothing here is specific to electrons.

**2. Where "not obtained" is stated too strongly or too weakly.** (Sect. 1.)

- **Too strongly:**
  - "The absence of energetic electrons is what such weak shocks give" (finding 7).
  - "The difficulty is computing size, not a missing idea" in Sect. 8 (finding 17).
- **Too weakly:**
  - Sect. 1, third bullet, says "one of them is numerical" as one explanation among six. With the registered population, the numerical explanation alone forbids any physical reading of the μ loss (finding 6). Sect. 1 should say that.
  - The same bullet reports the unregistered return shares (0–6 % against 1–15 %) inside the verdict section without the words "not registered". Sects. 6 and 9 have them; Sect. 1 needs them too.
- **Rightly:** "It does not say that a curved shock has no mechanism of its own" and "it says nothing about the designed Mach numbers".

**3. The registered threshold is very far from the realised shocks.** (Sects. 1, 5; protocol Sect. 6.)

- γ − 1 = 0.5 is about 44 (L) and 89 (M) times ½ m_e V_n² at the median V_n of the valid domain (0.150, 0.106). It is 2.8 (L) and 5.6 (M) times the ram energy of an incoming ion at m_i/m_e = 16 (reviewer's estimate).
- This threshold was set for the designed shocks. The γ 1.25–1.5 bin was then added after the data.
- The document should say this, because it is the honest form of "the runs could not have found it".
- A later protocol should register thresholds in units of ½ m_e V_n² (measured) and T_e, not in m_e c².

### C. Statements that are unsupported or overreach (ask 2)

**6. N3 is applied to the wrong population with a dwell the protocol did not register.** This is the most consequential error. (Sect. 6 explanation 1; Sect. 7 row C3; Sect. 8 item 4; Sect. 9 last bullet; protocol Sect. 5 N3; notebook, L-null entry.)

- **What the protocol registers:** ν τ for thermal electrons, with τ the measured width of the layer over V_n.
- **What the document computes:** 0.23–0.39 (L) and 0.11–0.19 (M). This multiplies the N3b rate of upstream electrons at γ 1.25–1.5 (9.4 × 10⁻⁴, 4.5 × 10⁻⁴) by the dwell of the all-energy returns, which are thermal.
- **What the thermal rate gives.** The Sect. 3 table has ν = 5.0 × 10⁻³ to 1.2 × 10⁻² for the bulk.
  - At the measured median dwell of 250 this gives ν τ ≈ 1.3–3.0 in L and about the same in M.
  - The L-null notebook entry already computed 0.33–0.55 at a dwell of 66 and predicted that B2 would carry the registered note.
  - The thermal mean free path is about 12 d_e ≈ 3 d_i (reviewer's estimate), which is comparable to the layer.
- **Sect. 9 is wrong on this point.**
  - It calls 66 and 113 "the protocol's assumed" dwell for N3. Those are the τ_dwell of B1′.
  - It says N3 "would pass" there (0.06, 0.05). For thermal electrons it fails at every dwell considered.
  - "None changes a registered criterion" is therefore not accurate.
- **The registered τ was never measured.** Explanation 2 says "the ramp width was not measured".
- **The note is missing.** B2 should carry the registered note that the loss cone is filled numerically.
- **Sect. 8 item 4 inherits the error** (finding 18).
- **Effect on the verdict:** it becomes firmer. The reading "most returns are not adiabatic mirror reflections" is, for now, a statement about the numerical floor.

**7. "The absence of energetic electrons is what such weak shocks give" is unsupported.** (Sect. 1, second bullet.)

- No theory or simulation is cited.
- The document's own baseline predicts 740–3890 (L) and 107–1046 (M) electrons at γ ≥ 1.5 for exactly these weak shocks. The sentence contradicts Sect. 6 unless it says why the baseline is wrong.
- What the evidence supports: the registered baseline predicted a beam at these shocks; the beam is absent; the baseline is outside its range of validity there (finding 8) and inside the numerical floor (finding 6). So the absence constrains neither the designed nor the realised Mach numbers.
- An analogy from a different regime, not a support: Kang, Ryu & Ha (2019, arXiv:1901.04173) find electron pre-acceleration only above M_s ≈ 2.3 in high-β quasi-perpendicular shocks.
- The first critical Mach number is not discussed. At fast Mach 1.5–2.5 and β ≈ 0.5–1.4 the valid shocks are probably sub-critical or marginal (Edmiston & Kennel 1984, from memory, not verified). That would mean little ion reflection and a laminar ramp, a regime different from that of the injection literature in `literature.md`.

**8. ε per band is not reported, although protocol Sect. 3.1 and R1 major issue 1 require it.**

- **Size of ε.** Reviewer's estimate with V_n = 0.15, R = 100 d_e, τ = 66: ε ≈ 0.14 (50°), 0.30 (60°), 0.75 (70°), 1.4 (75°). At the measured dwell it is about 3.8 times larger.
- **Where the beam would come from.** A cold electron reaches γ = 1.5 in one reflection only if V_HT ≳ 0.45, that is θ_Bn ≳ 70° at V_n = 0.15.
- **Consequence.** The whole predicted beam comes from the bands where the baseline's energy is not defined.
- **Sensitivity.** The reflected electrons there come from the far tail of the upstream Maxwellian (v_⊥ of several v_te). The predicted count is then exponentially sensitive to V_n, θ_Bn and φ. The document should give that sensitivity next to "missing by a factor of 200 to 500".

- **Not done:** B1′ was not re-run, and Sect. 9 does not list this.
- **S2.** The median dwell of 250 is 11 gyro-periods in L, above the 10 of the registered S2 dwell criterion. Bounce counts are not reported.

### D. Observations under-read (ask 3)

No observation is under-read as a hypothesis. Two are under-read as known physics that the existing data could close quantitatively.

**13. The returns look like thermal escape, and a count model for it is missing.** (Sect. 6 explanation 4; protocol Sect. 3.3.)

- **Zero-parameter estimate.** The fraction of a Maxwellian at T = 0.0105 with v_∥ > V_n is 7 % (L) and 15 % (M) (reviewer's estimate; an upper bound at low θ_Bn, since the foot moves at V_n/cos θ_Bn). The largest tracked shares are 9 % and 15 %.
- **What it reproduces:** the magnitude, the L–M ordering, the concentration at low θ_Bn and the small gain.
- **The controlling number is therefore V_n/v_te** (more precisely V_HT/v_te). Protocol C4 already names it; Sect. 7 says "not stated".
- **The regime.** Here V_n/v_te ≈ 1–1.5. That is the bow-shock or cluster-shock regime. A young remnant with upstream electrons near 10⁴ K has V_sh/v_te ≈ 8–13 (reviewer's estimate), so this channel would be exponentially small there. This limits what a later run at these parameters could say about the topic's purpose.
- **Already published:**
  - `savoini2010origin`: leaked electrons enter near 88° and leave near 48°; backstreaming electrons are 4.7 % of the interacting ones at M_A = 3, a number to compare with 1–9 % in L. Its "mirrored" class dwells about 2.7 gyro-periods and its "trapped" class about 40. The 7–11 gyro-periods here lie between them and are unlike a mirror.
  - `kraussvarban1994electron` (abstract only).
  - The Liouville-mapping literature on electron escape through the cross-shock potential (Feldman 1983; Scudder 1986; from memory, not verified).
- **A number left out of Sect. 6.** The records hold the foot angle at the deepest point of each return (10/50/90 % = 20/37/54° in L, 22/45/61° in M). The Sect. 6 table bins by θ_Bn at entry and compares a model that is static per foot with orbits that turn 30–40° lower.

**14. The decline of the piston-made energetic electrons is unexplained.**

- In L the count falls from 807 (t = 92.5) to 110 (t = 185) to ≤ 5 (t ≥ 296).
- Where the energy goes is not stated: adiabatic cooling in the expanding cavity, loss into another census class, or numerical drag.
- A referee would ask. The answer decides whether the 110 "downstream and layer" electrons at the start of the valid domain are driver remnants. That is the registered downstream branch of C1 (finding 20).

**15. The early energetic electrons are a piston effect, not a shock result.**

- The notebook gives parallel work of about 1 %, so the gain is perpendicular. The electrons appear in the current layer at the edge of the diamagnetic cavity, and their number orders with Ω_ce/ω_pe.
- Known in the laser-plasma literature: `lezhnin2021kinetic`; Niemann et al. 2013; Winske & Gary 2007 (the last two from memory, not verified).
- The effect would vanish at realistic ω_pe/Ω_ce. The document should name it so and cite the note.

### E. Sect. 8, "What a run would need" (ask 4)

**16. Item 3 uses the wrong length and the wrong requirement.**

- **"R_m well above 20 d_i" ignores M_A.**
  - The published formation criteria are in the debris gyroradius ρ_d = V_d/Ω_ci = M_A d_i and in times of order Ω_ci⁻¹ (Clark et al. 2014, Phys. Rev. E 90, 041101, verified; Phys. Plasmas 20, 082129, title verified, authors and year from memory; APS DPP 2014 abstract on onset time and coupling parameter).
  - Here R_m/ρ_d = 4.6 (L), 1.9 (M), 1.0 (H) (reviewer's estimate). H formed no shock. This one number orders the three runs.
- **The two runs cannot choose between readings.** The first valid front appears at:
  - the same radius in d_i (18 in both);
  - the same fraction of R_B (0.49 and 0.48);
  - nearly the same time in Ω_ci⁻¹ (3.3 and 2.7).

  The document picks the first. If the third holds, the need is R_m ≳ 3–5 ρ_d, which is 18–30 d_i at M_A = 6 and not 20 d_i. The item should state all three and say which run would separate them.
- **"Valid while the piston drives" is not a physical requirement.** A decaying blast wave is a legitimate shock, and the Sedov phase is the remnant's own. Register instead: fast Mach ≥ X for ≥ N Ω_ci⁻¹ inside the valid domain, with X above the critical Mach number.

**17. "Items 3, 4 and 6 pull against each other at fixed memory" is fair but incomplete, and "computing size, not a missing idea" overreaches.**

- **The conflict between 3 and 6 is physical, not only one of memory.**
  - Adiabaticity at the ramp is (m_i/m_e)/M_A.
  - The box in d_e grows as M_A √(m_i/m_e).
  - Example (reviewer's estimate): with R_m ≈ 5 ρ_d and a half-width of 2–3 R_m, M_A = 6 at m_i/m_e = 64 needs a half-width of 480–720 d_e. That is 16–50 times the cells of 768³ at dx = 0.5, and four times more again at 8 particles per cell.
  - The document should carry such a table, so that "computing size" is a shown statement.
- **A cheaper design exists and is not named: 2.5-D.**
  - A cylindrical piston with the field in the plane is the geometry of `savoini2001twodimensional` and `savoini2010origin`.
  - At the memory of 768³ × 2 it allows, for example, 8192² cells at 13 particles per cell.
  - It could test the finite-ε baseline, a leakage count model and the convergence of ν τ with particles per cell, at M_A and m_i/m_e beyond those papers (3 and 42–84).
  - It cannot test K1: with an ignorable coordinate, particles stay tied to their field lines (Jokipii, Kóta & Giacalone 1993, GRL 20, 1759; abstract verified).
  - So 2-D suits the baseline and the floor, and 3-D is for what only 3-D can show.
- **Another design to name:** a plane, driven shock running into a field with large-scale curvature. It gives the same foot kinematics at a controlled Mach number (compare `kong2016electron`).
- Whether the engine supports either design, I did not judge.
- **More than computing is missing.** With unlimited memory the study would still lack:
  - a narrowed question (README);
  - a controlling number with a threshold;
  - a baseline valid at ε ~ 1.

**18. Item 4 is for the wrong population.**

- "ν lower by 2.3–3.9 … about 8 per cell" follows from the γ 1.25–1.5 rate.
- For thermal electrons at the measured dwell the factor is about 12–30. That is some 25–60 particles per cell if ν ∝ 1/ppc.
- That scaling is unmeasured. ν also falls with the lag in the Sect. 3 table, so the long-lag rate is not known either.

**19. The test-particle baseline of item 2 is unsound as written.**

- **Cadence.**
  - The slices are 18.5 apart, about one electron gyro-period in L (22 upstream, about 11 at b = 2).
  - The front moves 2.8 d_e (L) and 2.0 d_e (M) between slices, against a ramp of at most about one d_i.
  - Linear interpolation in the lab frame makes the ramp smear and re-form once per slice interval, near the gyro-frequency. That breaks μ artificially.
- **Noise.** The 2-particle noise of one slice is frozen for 18.5 instead of about 1/ω_pe. Unsmoothed, the test particles scatter more than the electrons of the run, not less.
- **What it needs:**
  - interpolation in the front frame (ξ = r − R(θ, t) from the fit);
  - smoothing along the front and over the eight half-planes, never across the ramp;
  - a stated kernel;
  - two null tests: the same processing on L-null slices, and an analytic translating ramp sampled every 18.5;
  - a decision rule fixed in advance for both outcomes.
- **The logic is asymmetric.**
  - If the test particles reproduce the returns, explanations 2–6 suffice.
  - If they do not, fluctuations remain, numerical or physical, and N6 is still needed.
- **Axisymmetric fields** conserve the canonical angular momentum, so cross-field transport and ripples are excluded by construction. This must be stated.
- **Either way it validates a baseline and yields no hypothesis.** The analyses of finding 21 are cheaper and come first.
